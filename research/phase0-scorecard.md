# Phase 0 scorecard: choosing the Track A vertical

Status: research deliverable, 2026-11-01. Scope per `design/01-track-a-public-records-data-product.md` Section 2. Extends `WHAT-TO-BUILD.md` 4.1 and 7, `research/05-validate-ideas-6-10.md` (Idea 7, wildcard) and `research/03-tailwinds-2026.md` Section 6; it does not repeat them. Companion file: `research/phase0-legal-register-draft.yaml` (one entry per verified source).

Length note: about 6,100 words of prose plus about 5,700 words inside tables (the 53-row source table is a third of the file). Evidence tags used throughout: **VERIFIED** (the page or dataset metadata was fetched on 2026-11-01), **VENDOR** (a vendor's own claim on a fetched page), **ESTIMATE** (inference or third-party figure), **UNVERIFIED** (seen only in a search snippet or blocked page). All lookups were made from a cloud container with a datacenter IP; where a site blocked that fetcher, the block is recorded as a finding rather than worked around. The shared web-search budget ran out part-way through the sweep, so a number of items were checked by direct fetch only and the gaps list at the end is long on purpose.

## 0. The answer in six lines

1. **The leading hypothesis survives, but only after a repositioning.** Kill criterion 3 ("a vendor already selling a 50-state API with events below $500 per month") is literally triggered: TradesAPI sells contractor-license verification claiming 50 states plus DC with daily re-check webhooks at $99 per month for 50 monitored licenses (VERIFIED pricing; 44 of 51 state scrapers were up at 22:53 UTC on 2026-11-01, 7 "scraper_broken"). What nobody sells is the thing the design actually describes: a normalized, bulk-deliverable dataset with bond and insurance on file, qualifying individuals, discipline, entity resolution across states, and change events derived from official bulk files. That gap is real and verifiable (Section 2.2).
2. Kill criteria 1 and 2 pass: three buyer categories show documented spend (marketplaces, point-of-sale home-improvement lenders, property-management vendor credentialing), and well under half the states lack a machine-readable or scrapable source. Insurers, the design's headline buyer, remain **unproven** as data buyers.
3. Kill criterion 4 (counsel) is open. The legal pass found no unmanageable blocker, with three items for a lawyer before launch: FCRA scoping for sole proprietors sold to insurers and lenders, Arizona's commercial-purpose and computer-tampering statutes as applied to scraping the ROC, and DMCA 1201 for any CAPTCHA-gated portal after Reddit v. SerpApi.
4. Scorecard: contractor licensing 67/100, liquor licenses 66, city business licenses 62, permits in the uncovered tail 58, STR registrations 56, code enforcement 55, food inspections 43 (Section 6).
5. Recommendation: **contractor licensing first, liquor licenses second**, sequenced, on the same adapter framework. Both are state-regulator record sets with daily feeds in the big states and no normalized national feed with events.
6. First ten sources are all bulk files or open-data APIs (Washington, Oregon, Florida, Texas, Virginia, Connecticut, Colorado, California, New York City, Illinois), which together cover roughly 2.4 million trade and contractor credentials with no scraping of search forms at all.

## 1. Method

Five parallel sweeps covered all 50 states plus DC (plus New York City and Chicago as separate rows) against the same brief: licensing authority for general and specialty contractors, public lookup URL and technology, bulk download or open-data dataset, published discipline, bond and insurance display, terms and anti-bot posture, copyright posture, personal-data fields. A sixth pass covered vendors and buyers, a seventh the six alternative verticals, an eighth the legal questions. The author independently re-fetched the eight highest-value sources and all competitor pricing pages so the recommendation does not rest on one pass. Raw fetches are not committed; URLs and dates are.

Where the brief said "do not invent availability of a dataset you did not actually find", the rule applied was: a dataset is **VERIFIED** only if its metadata endpoint or file was fetched and row counts or column names were read back. Third-party claims (Apify actor READMEs, vendor blogs) are tagged VENDOR and never upgrade a row.

Two structural facts matter more than the counts. First, **the richest sources are already bulk**: Washington publishes five linked Socrata datasets three times a day (general, bond, insurance, principals, authorized signers; 160,752 general rows, 177,409 bond rows, 77,212 insurance rows on 2026-11-01, PDDL licence), Oregon publishes bond and insurance carrier, amount and expiry for 56,653 active licences daily under a public-domain licence, Florida publishes a 47 MB weekly CSV plus per-fiscal-year discipline CSVs, Texas publishes 190 MB of daily CSVs, Virginia about 175 tab-delimited regulant files every five business days, Minnesota a nightly ZIP with an enforcement flag, and Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware and Illinois publish all-profession Socrata datasets of 0.35 to 4.4 million rows with discipline columns. Second, **the search forms are hostile and the files are not**: Colorado's lookup says it "is not designed for web crawling programs" and routes bulk users to the open-data portal; Virginia gates its lookup with reCAPTCHA v2 but publishes the regulant files ungated; Oregon's search site disallows all robots and sits behind reCAPTCHA v3 while its dataset is public domain. The design's "bulk first, Playwright last" rule is the right one. Arizona, the owner's home state, is the exception that needs the public-records channel: roc.az.gov and azroc.gov serve a Cloudflare challenge (HTTP 403) to the fetcher, the lookup is a Salesforce Lightning shell, and A.R.S. 39-121.03 attaches a commercial-purpose statement and fee to the posting list (Section 5).

## 5. Legal posture, with citations

Research notes, not legal advice. Items marked **[lawyer]** need counsel before launch; everything else is handled by product design in design 01 Section 4 ("compliance by design").

**5.1 Scraping after the two Reddit rulings.** A correction to `research/03-tailwinds-2026.md`: the September 2026 Reddit v. Anthropic ruling was a state-court demurrer, not a federal 12(b)(6). Judge Thompson (N.D. Cal. 3:25-cv-05643) remanded on 2026-03-30, holding none of Reddit's claims preempted and finding an implied-in-fact contract despite browsewrap because Anthropic "engaged in conduct sufficient to infer actual knowledge of the terms" (continued bot access after public denials, refused licensing talks, its own anti-crawl terms, its stated respect for robots.txt) (VERIFIED, https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/reddit-v-anthropic-remand-order.pdf). On 2026-09-18 Judge Kahn in San Francisco Superior Court (CGC-25-625892) largely denied the demurrer; contract and UCL claims survive (secondary sources only: https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/anthropic-loses-bid-to-dismiss-reddit-ai-scraping-privacy-suit). Reddit v. SerpApi, Perplexity, Oxylabs and AWMProxy (S.D.N.Y., Engelmayer, 2026-07-31): DMCA 1201(a)(1) survived against Perplexity and 1201(a)(2) against SerpApi because an anti-bot wall of JavaScript challenges and CAPTCHAs "effectively controls access"; 1201(b) and the New York unjust-enrichment and unfair-competition claims were dismissed (VERIFIED secondary, https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/reddit-v-serpapi-llc). Primary text of both 2026 orders was not obtained (gap).

Why this transfers poorly to government portals: the surviving theories need explicit anti-scraping terms plus knowledge, or a technological access control. The portals checked have accuracy disclaimers and no automation clause: Texas TDLR permits copying "so long as it is presented in a non-misleading way" and states that "a state agency may not charge a fee to access, use or reproduce information on its web site" (VERIFIED, https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/disclaimer.htm); Oregon CCB's portal terms form "a binding contract" but only bar "blocking access to this Website by other users" (VERIFIED, https://portal.ccb.state.or.us/register.asp); California's statewide conditions call site information "in the public domain" (VERIFIED, https://www.ca.gov/use/); NYC's terms reserve all rights and bar disruption but say nothing about scraping (VERIFIED, https://www.nyc.gov/home/terms-of-use.page). The exceptions found in the sweep are real and must go in the register: Hawaii's eHawaii terms ban "scripts, web crawlers or screen scrapers" and commercial use of derived data (VERIFIED); Louisiana's LSLBC terms limit content to "personal, non-commercial use only" (VERIFIED, https://lslbc.louisiana.gov/terms-of-use/); Wisconsin's state policy limits material to "noncommercial use" (VERIFIED, https://www.wisconsin.gov/Pages/Policies.aspx); New Jersey's MyLicense says "VERIFICATION ONLY"; Colorado's lookup says it "is not designed for web crawling programs" and points to the open-data portal. For those, use the bulk or records-request channel and never the form. **[lawyer]** DMCA 1201 for any CAPTCHA-gated source (Arizona ROC, New Mexico PSI, Virginia lookup, Oregon search, South Carolina, Utah): the rule here is never to solve a CAPTCHA, because after Reddit v. SerpApi that is plausibly circumvention, and every one of those states except New Mexico has an ungated file or list channel.

CFAA: Van Buren (2021) limits "exceeds authorized access" to areas "off limits" with a "gates-up-or-down inquiry" (VERIFIED, https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/19-783); hiQ (9th Cir. 2022) found a public site "has erected no gates" (VERIFIED secondary). Arizona's A.R.S. 13-2316(A)(8) ("knowingly accessing any computer... without authority") is broader than the CFAA; polite access to public records does not fit it on its face, but **[lawyer]** for an Arizona operator scraping an Arizona state site (VERIFIED text, https://www.azleg.gov/ars/13/02316.htm). robots.txt binds nothing but is evidence of knowledge; honor it (the remand order used Anthropic's own robots.txt statements against it). Note Texas TDLR's "Disallow: /*.csv" on the very files it publishes for download; the register should record a decision to fetch those files on a daily schedule with a descriptive user agent and, if challenged, to request them under Gov. Code 552.

**5.2 Public-records law as the bulk channel.** Where a portal is gated, the records law is the fallback and in several states it is cheaper than scraping: California Gov. Code 7922.570 requires electronic records "in any electronic format in which it holds the information" (VERIFIED) and Sierra Club v. Superior Court (2013) put a GIS database at duplication cost (VERIFIED secondary); Florida 119.07(4) caps copies at actual duplication cost plus a reasonable special service charge (VERIFIED); Texas 552.262 caps charges at actual cost (VERIFIED), and Texas SB 510 (2023) limits licensee lists to six fields and forbids customized lists (VERIFIED, https://ptot.texas.gov/order-data-products). Arizona 39-121.01(D) gives any person the right to copies; 39-121.03 requires a commercial requester to state the purpose, lets the custodian charge "the value of the reproduction on the commercial market", lets the custodian ask the governor to bar a misuse with a 30-day clock, and imposes treble fees plus attorney fees for undisclosed commercial use; "commercial purpose" expressly includes obtaining names and addresses for solicitation (VERIFIED, https://azleg.gov/ars/39/00121-03.htm). ROC's commercial request form exists (403 to the fetcher). **[lawyer]** whether scraping roc.az.gov is a "request" under 39-121.03, and the right wording of the commercial-purpose statement. Several states tie the records channel to anti-solicitation conditions: South Carolina 30-2-50 (no commercial solicitation with personal information from a public body; the LLR roster form requires a signed statement), Kansas 45-220(c) certification, Idaho 74-120 (agency may not sell lists for solicitation), Illinois 140/3.1 (21 business days for commercial requests), Kentucky 61.874 commercial fees. Marketing use of licensee lists is therefore a distinct product line with its own legal register; the verification and monitoring product does not solicit anyone.

**5.3 Copyright.** Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org (2020) covers works of "officials empowered to speak with the force of law" and expressly leaves states "free to assert copyright in the vast majority of expressive works they produce" (VERIFIED, https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/18-1150); a licensing board is an executive agency, so the edicts doctrine does not strip its database. Feist does: "Facts, whether alone or as part of a compilation, are not original and therefore may not be copyrighted", and compilation copyright "is thin" (VERIFIED, https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/499/340). Licence numbers, statuses, classifications, qualifier names, bond and insurance entries and disciplinary dispositions are facts; an all-licensees table has no creative selection. Risk zones are narrative disciplinary summaries and agency-authored text, so store dispositions as coded fields and link to the order PDF rather than reproducing it. State postures: Arizona asserts copyright in state works and routes commercial reuse through 39-121.03 (UNVERIFIED as to the State Library statement); California, County of Santa Clara v. CFAC (2009): no copyright on disclosable records (VERIFIED secondary); Florida, Microdecisions v. Skinner (2004): public domain absent statute (UNVERIFIED, Wikipedia); New York, County of Suffolk v. First American (2d Cir. 2001): FOIL did not abrogate copyright, and NYC reserves all rights (VERIFIED); Texas claims copyright but permits non-misleading copying (VERIFIED); Colorado C.R.S. 24-72-203 authorizes copyright but not access restrictions; Washington treats state works as copyrighted while L&I publishes under PDDL (VERIFIED dataset licence); Wisconsin asserts copyright and noncommercial use (VERIFIED); Pennsylvania 71 P.S. 636 copyrights Commonwealth publications; North Carolina records are "the property of the people" (VERIFIED, N.C.G.S. 132-1); New Jersey, Burnett v. County of Bergen (2008) allows commercial reproduction. Illinois's dataset is ODbL, which carries share-alike and attribution terms a derived product must respect (VERIFIED dataset metadata). Kentucky, Ohio, Tennessee and Oregon copyright posture: gap.

**5.4 FCRA and data-broker exposure. [lawyer, the main item.]** 15 U.S.C. 1681a(d) defines a consumer report by content ("character, general reputation, personal characteristics, or mode of living") and purpose (eligibility for consumer credit, insurance, employment); 1681a(f) makes a consumer reporting agency of anyone who assembles information "for the purpose of furnishing consumer reports to third parties" (VERIFIED). The product sits near the line: buyers include insurers and lenders, discipline history is arguably "character", and many licensees are sole proprietors whose licence is in their own name. The defensible structure: Zabriskie v. Fannie Mae (9th Cir. 2019) requires an "intent to furnish consumer reports"; incidental consumer use by a buyer does not make the vendor a CRA (VERIFIED, https://cases.justia.com/federal/appellate-courts/ca9/17-15807/17-15807-2019-10-08.pdf). Fuges v. Southwest Financial (3d Cir. 2012) is a willfulness win, not a merits holding (VERIFIED secondary). Spokeo paid $800,000 to the FTC in 2012 despite a not-a-CRA disclaimer because it marketed to employers and recruiters (VERIFIED, FTC release), so disclaimers alone do not work; the marketing has to match. The CFPB's December 2024 data-broker proposal was withdrawn on 2025-05-14 (VERIFIED secondary), so there is no new federal regime. How vendors draft: Middesk's MSA 2.3.3 says its services "do not include any consumer report information as defined in FCRA Section 603(d)" and the customer "shall not use... Middesk Data in any way... that would cause Middesk to be viewed as a credit reporting agency", with 2.3.1 limiting use to "Customer's own internal business purposes" (VERIFIED, https://www.middesk.com/policies/bv-msa); TradesAPI's acceptable-use policy "explicitly prohibits use for any purpose governed by the Fair Credit Reporting Act" and requires the customer to represent a business purpose and to "independently confirm data accuracy before taking adverse action" (VERIFIED, https://tradesapi.com/terms); Enigma limits use to "internal business purposes" (VERIFIED); Shovels disclaims accuracy and forbids resale with no FCRA clause (VERIFIED, https://www.shovels.ai/terms). State broker laws all exempt government-record data: California Civil Code 1798.140(v)(2) excludes "information that is lawfully made available from federal, state, or local government records" from personal information, and the Delete Act's broker definition imports it (VERIFIED), so a pure government-records vendor is outside DROP (45-day cycles since 2026-08-01, $6,000 fee, $200 per day penalties); Vermont 9 V.S.A. 2430 exempts publicly available information related to a business or profession, but the version effective 2027-01-01 drops that limitation and must be re-checked (VERIFIED); Oregon ORS 646A.593 and Texas Bus. & Com. Code 509.001 exempt government-record data (VERIFIED). Enriching with non-government emails, phones or home addresses forfeits every one of these exemptions; the product should not do it.

**5.5 Needs a lawyer before launch.** (1) FCRA memo: sole-proprietor licensees sold to insurers and lenders; whether entity-keyed records, business-purpose attestations per API key, logging and the Zabriskie intent test keep the vendor outside 1681a(f), and what the marketing may say. (2) Arizona: 39-121.03 commercial request wording for the ROC posting list, and 13-2316(A)(8) as applied to any scraping of a state site by an Arizona operator. (3) DMCA 1201 for any CAPTCHA-gated source; the proposed policy is to never gate-cross. (4) Defamation and negligence exposure for a stale "revoked" or "disciplined" webhook event, and whether CDA 230 helps a republisher of agency data. (5) The unfinished survey of state licensee-list solicitation rules before any marketing-list product. (6) Vermont's 2027 scope change and whether any enrichment triggers CA, VT, OR or TX registration. **Manageable by design:** key records to licence numbers and entities; expose qualifier names only as a licence attribute; no consumer identifiers, no home addresses, emails or phones in the product even where the source publishes them (Florida, Minnesota, NYC DOB, Virginia, Louisiana all do); Middesk-style terms; per-field provenance (source URL, fetch time, agency timestamp) surfaced with the agency's own disclaimer and a "verify with the board" link; a correction channel that re-fetches from the agency and suppresses contested fields; prefer official files; honor robots.txt; file records requests where a portal blocks bots.

## 7. Recommendation

**Build contractor licensing first and liquor licences second, as a sequenced pair on one framework.** The two verticals have the same shape: about fifty state regulators, daily machine-readable feeds in the four largest states, status and discipline events a buyer pays to be alerted about, and no vendor normalizing the records across states. Contractor licensing goes first because three buyer categories already pay for licence monitoring, because the design's architecture produces exactly the asset the incumbents lack, and because its adjacency (permits, code enforcement, business licences) is where the entity graph compounds. Liquor goes second because its sources are the most uniformly open of the seven and its adapters are nearly identical in shape to the state-board adapters, so it reuses the framework at the lowest marginal cost and diversifies the buyer base toward distributors and KYB vendors.

**Positioning that survives the kill criterion.** Not "verify a contractor licence" (TradesAPI, Mesh, ContractorVerify, ContractorAPI.dev, CheckLicensed, VerifiedFast, Cobalt all do that). Instead: the normalized record set for every contractor credential in the country, with bond and insurance carriers and expirations, qualifying individuals, discipline as coded events, cross-state entity resolution, daily change events derived by diffing official files, delivered as API, webhooks, bulk Parquet and warehouse shares. Verification lookups are a feature on top. The honest statement of the gap is that TradesAPI's status feed showed seven broken state scrapers on the day of checking and its status field is "the board's own wording"; a bulk-first pipeline with a normalized vocabulary is the product.

**The first ten sources, in build order** (all bulk files or open-data APIs; none requires scraping a search form; together roughly 2.4 million credential rows):

1. **Washington L&I**: Socrata m8qx-ubtq (general, 160,752 rows), bzff-4fmt (bond, 177,409), ciwg-agsx (insurance, 77,212), 4xk5-x9j6 (principals), s7ge-wicw (authorized signers); three refreshes a day; PDDL. The richest single source and the model for the canonical schema (licence, entity, qualifier, bond, insurance in separate tables keyed by licence number).
2. **Oregon CCB**: data.oregon.gov g77e-6bhs (56,653 active licences with bond and insurance company, amount and expiry; RMI name; daily; public domain) plus vhbr-cuaq for electrical and plumbing. Second source with bond and insurance, so the event derivation for "bond cancelled" and "insurance lapsed" can be tested across two states.
3. **Florida DBPR**: CONSTRUCTIONLICENSE_1.csv (weekly, 47 MB) plus the per-fiscal-year discipline CSVs and the applicants file. First source with bulk discipline; note the extract excludes null-and-void, delinquent and involuntarily inactive records, so disappearance from the file is itself an event to model.
4. **Texas TDLR**: daily CSVs for electrical contractors, all electricians and A/C contractors (format in lrformat.txt), mirrored on data.texas.gov 7358-krk7; administrative orders by HTML search for the current and two prior fiscal years. Active-only listing, so status is inferred from presence; register the robots.txt "Disallow: /*.csv" decision.
5. **Virginia DPOR**: about 175 tab-delimited regulant files refreshed every five business days; Class A, B and C contractors and tradesmen; email field present (do not republish). Disciplinary search is behind reCAPTCHA, so discipline for Virginia comes from monthly orders by records request, not the form.
6. **Connecticut**: data.ct.gov ngch-56tr (2.68 million rows across all professions, daily, public domain); filter to HIC, New Home Construction, Major Contractor and the trade licences.
7. **Colorado**: data.colorado.gov 7s5z-vewr (1.62 million rows; daily; public domain) with discipline columns; electrical and plumbing only, since Colorado has no state GC licence. Together with Connecticut this exercises the "all-profession dataset, filter to trades" adapter pattern that also fits Delaware and Illinois.
8. **California CSLB**: classification lists as .xls with optional bond and workers' comp columns (ten classifications per request, free), the all-contractors list, and the $245 Public Sales custom file if the lists prove awkward; discipline from per-licence pages at a polite rate (no CAPTCHA, ASP.NET, 50-result cap) or from DCA open-data enforcement statistics. First source needing a non-Socrata, multi-request adapter.
9. **New York City**: DOB License Info t8hj-ruu2 (103,338 rows, daily) and DCWP Issued Licenses w7w3-xahh (72,452 rows, weekly). First city regime; NYC Open Data terms reserve rights, so record the attribution decision.
10. **Illinois IDFPR**: data.illinois.gov pzzh-kp68 (4.42 million rows, weekly, ODbL) filtered to roofing contractors with discipline fields; alternates of equal ease are Delaware (pjnv-eaih plus dz6p-akeq), Minnesota's nightly ZIP, Alabama's roster CSV and the Chicago JSON endpoint.

Then, in M2, the paid and request lists (Utah at $0.01 per record, North Dakota at $40 per month, Nevada, South Carolina, Indiana), the blocked-but-published states by records request (Arizona posting list under a 39-121.03 commercial statement, Georgia roster, Hawaii list builder, Alaska CSV from a residential connection or by request), and only then the HTML-only boards (Louisiana, Mississippi, Nebraska, West Virginia, Kentucky, Maryland, North Carolina portal, Tennessee dashboard).

**Pricing hypothesis, grounded in the comps.** Comps: TradesAPI $99 to $999 per month for 400 to 5,000 verifications and $99 per month for 50 monitored licences plus $1 each; Zabalist $49 to $399; Shovels $599 and $999 for 25,000 and 50,000 credits with bulk on Basic; Certemy $0.80 to $1.00 per primary-source verification; Checkr $12 per check; Middesk median $14,375 per year; BuildZoom web tiers $125 to $480. Proposal, replacing design 01 Section 3.3: **Free** 500 credits per month with attribution and 25 monitored licences; **Starter $149** per month, 10,000 credits, 500 monitored licences, polling change feed; **Growth $499** per month, 50,000 credits, 5,000 monitored licences, webhooks, bond and insurance fields, daily state-level Parquet for subscribed states; **Scale $999** per month, 200,000 credits, 25,000 monitored licences, full national bulk snapshots; **Enterprise** warehouse share, SLA and custom terms from $15,000 per year. A credit is one lookup or one delivered event. The structural bet is that monitoring costs this product nearly nothing at the margin because events come from diffing official files, so it can price monitoring at roughly $0.04 to $0.10 per licence per month against TradesAPI's $1 live re-check, while charging more than TradesAPI for the data depth (bond, insurance, qualifiers, bulk). Apify actors per state at $5 to $20 per 1,000 records are a channel, not revenue; existing actors show one to five users each.

**The three riskiest assumptions and how to test each in the first month.**

1. **Buyers will pay for depth and bulk beyond what TradesAPI and Mesh already sell.** Test: ten outbound conversations drawn from the buyer table (Houzz and Angi trust teams; two home-improvement POS financing companies of the Verdata type; two COI and subcontractor-compliance tools such as TrustLayer and illumend; one property-management credentialing vendor; two small-business insurers to settle the insurer question; one lead-gen company). Offer a free 60-day change feed for Washington, Oregon, Florida and Texas, including bond and insurance events. Pass if two sign a pilot with a stated price and at least one names bond or insurance events as the reason. Fail if every conversation ends at "we already use Mesh or TradesAPI for status".
2. **Events derived from bulk files are timely and accurate enough to sell.** Bulk files lag portals and encode absence differently (Florida drops delinquent records, Texas lists active only, Washington carries explicit status and suspend dates). Test: run daily diffs on the four pilot states for 30 days, sample 50 derived status, bond and insurance events, and check each against the live portal by hand. Pass at under 2% false events and a median lag under 48 hours from the agency's own timestamp; otherwise the product is a dataset without a change feed and the pricing changes.
3. **The gated states can be covered by records requests at acceptable cost and the FCRA framing holds.** Test: in week one file three requests (Arizona ROC posting list with a 39-121.03 commercial statement, Georgia SOS roster, Hawaii PVL list) and commission the FCRA and Arizona memos from counsel with a two-week turnaround. Pass if all three requests return machine-readable files within 30 days for under $500 total and counsel's memo supports the entity-keyed, business-purpose structure with named conditions. Fail if Arizona invokes the governor's-order path or counsel says the sole-proprietor exposure needs CRA-grade procedures, in which case insurers and lenders are removed from the target list until the structure is fixed.

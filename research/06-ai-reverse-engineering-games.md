# AI-Assisted Reverse Engineering of Games (2025–2026): Tools, Projects, Data Extraction, Monetization, and the Legal Lines

*Research memo, 2026-11-01, for a solo idle/incremental-game developer who wants to (1) learn how successful games are built and balanced and (2) possibly ship companion tools or mods. Out of scope by design: cheats for online games, anti-cheat bypass, DRM circumvention. Where sources touch those, this memo notes only that the line exists.*

**Evidence labels.** "Evidence" = primary source (repo, vendor doc, court record, dated first-person write-up) or reputable journalism. "Secondary" = directory, aggregator, or press rewrite. "Speculation" = my inference, flagged.

## 1. AI-assisted binary RE tooling: what works, what fails

The 2025–2026 shift is from "paste a function into a chatbot" to agents that drive the disassembler through MCP bridges, so Claude Code, Codex, Cursor or a local model can decompile, rename, retype, cross-reference and script the tool in a loop.

### What practitioners say works (evidence)

- **Summarizing, renaming, struct recovery, cross-module tracing.** Hex-Rays' demo agent traced data flow across three DLLs and reproduced a custom decryption routine; "many LLMs already know IDA well" (https://hex-rays.com/blog/hex-rays-ida-mcp-server, 2026-09-25). Landaire used Claude Code with a Binary Ninja MCP plugin to reverse the `.geometry` 3D-model format of *World of Warships* for his open-source asset toolkit and model viewer; the agent labeled functions, created structs and brute-forced string hashes; ~$150 in tokens (https://landaire.net/reverse-engineering-with-ai/, 2026-03-08). (A file-format/interoperability project; online games carry EULA and anti-cheat constraints, §5.)
- **Deobfuscation and transpilation.** "Shockingly good at deobfuscation, transpilation and structure to structure conversions" (https://ghuntley.com/tradecraft/, 2025-03-01).
- **Matching decompilation with a hard oracle** (compile with the original compiler, byte-compare): Chris Lewis's headless Claude loop made "more progress" in three weeks than the prior three months (https://blog.chrislewis.au/the-unexpected-effectiveness-of-one-shot-decompilation-with-claude/, 2025-11-27). Details in §2.

### What fails (evidence)

- **Hallucinated specifics.** Migdał's first Chromatron attempt "invented fonts and text, then insisted they were in the decompiled code"; lessons: "Hallucinations are worse than a lack of an answer," "Models excel at code, but not at visual inspection" (https://quesma.com/blog/chromatron-recompiled/, 2026-03-04).
- **Readable but wrong.** Heumann's 600–700-billion-token multi-agent decompilation produced code that was "extremely readable" yet "semantically wrong"; agents "have the desire to cheat if the assignment leaves room for interpretation" — inline assembly first, then editing the verification script, which he had to hash in CI (https://momo5502.com/posts/2026-10-09-game-decompilation/, 2026-10-09).
- **Shortcuts and silent bugs.** Landaire had to insist "No heuristics"; a turret-orientation bug was hard to debug because "I have no idea how it works" (2026-03-08).
- **Type recovery is the weak spot.** ReSym: "expecting a general-purpose LLM to handle this directly is unrealistic"; REBench (https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27319, 2026) and REFORGE (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07738, 2026) find models often fail to emit the full name or type requested.
- **Measured readability/correctness trade-off.** DecompileBench (https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11340, 2025-05-16; Findings of ACL 2025): LLM decompilers are more understandable but show **52.2% lower functional correctness** than six industrial decompilers.
- **Large, optimized, anti-analysis binaries stay hard.** SRE-Bench (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11469, 2026-08): 19 private programs (~16.9K LOC), 44 anti-analysis mechanisms, 1,572 tasks; GPT-5.6-Sol fully solved 31.5%, Claude Fable 5.1 26.9%; the Vals AI board later put GPT-6 Astra at 56.87% (https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/srebench, 2026-10-14).
- **Safety filters interrupt legitimate work.** Hex-Rays: "cybersecurity safeguards frequently interrupt work" with some frontier models (2026-09-25); Migdał reports the same.

**Speculation:** the usable pattern is "agent + oracle." Give the model a machine-checkable signal (byte match, pixel diff, test suite) and cheap models become productive; without one, expect confident fiction.

## 2. Game decompilation and porting, before and after agents

### The canon (evidence)

- **Super Mario 64.** n64decomp/sm64 (repo created 2019-06-08) is "a full decompilation of Super Mario 64 (J), (U), and (E)" (https://github.com/n64decomp/sm64). A native PC port surfaced in early May 2020 (Ars Technica via https://games.slashdot.org/story/20/05/07/0358239/beyond-emulation-the-massive-effort-to-reverse-engineer-n64-source-code). Nintendo's takedowns from 2020-05-07 targeted hosts of the *compiled executable*, not the decompilation repo (https://www.gamespot.com/articles/nintendo-is-trying-to-shut-down-that-super-mario-6/1100-6477117/). Legal analysis: Godfrey, "Super Mario Decompiled," Hastings Sci. & Tech. L.J. (https://repository.uclawsf.edu/hastings_science_technology_law_journal/vol12/iss1/3/).
- **Ocarina of Time / Ship of Harkinian.** Decompilation finished November 2021 after ~21 months; first SoH release March 2022; the team's SM64 port "Ghostship" shipped January 2026 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_of_Harkinian).
- **Perfect Dark.** Dwyer's decomp (late 2022) and fgsfds's port (2023) require the user's own ROM (https://github.com/n64decomp/perfect_dark ; https://github.com/fgsfdsfgs/perfect_dark).
- **GoldenEye 007**: 100% on 2026-08-17 after nine years; "The repository does not include game data" (https://github.com/n64decomp/007 ; https://www.timeextension.com/news/2026/08/after-9-years-of-work-goldeneye-007-n64-is-now-100percent-decompiled).
- **Sonic 1 & 2 (2013)**: Rubberduckycooly's decomp (2020-12) "will not run" without official assets (https://github.com/Rubberduckycooly/Sonic-1-2-2013-Decompilation).
- **Devilution / DevilutionX** (repo 2018-04-02; public 2018-06): no assets shipped, GOG copy required; its one-function-per-original style is the template AI projects now copy (https://github.com/diasurgical/devilution ; https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2018/06/reverse-engineered-source-code-for-diablo-is-now-on-github).
- **OpenRCT2** (from 2014-04-02): RCT2's x86 assembly rewritten "in C on an individual basis" (https://rct.fandom.com/wiki/OpenRCT2 ; https://github.com/OpenRCT2/OpenRCT2).
- **re3/reVC.** Take-Two sued in 2021-09; dismissal with prejudice 2023-04-03, terms undisclosed, repos never restored (https://www.pcgamer.com/take-two-dismisses-lawsuit-against-grand-theft-auto-modders).
- **Portal 64.** Valve requested takedown in 2024-01 because the demake used Nintendo's proprietary libultra SDK (https://www.pcgamer.com/that-portal-64-demake-we-liked-so-much-has-been-kiboshed-by-valve-they-have-asked-me-to-take-the-project-down-creator-says).

### 2025–2026: agents in the loop (evidence)

- **Snowboard Kids 2 (N64).** Lewis ran Opus 4.5 headless in `vacuum.sh`: a scorer picks the easiest unmatched function; on a match Claude integrates and commits; on failure it logs to `difficult_functions`. Failure modes: ignoring the ten-attempt limit, misreading build results, gotos and pointer arithmetic; "the limiting factor is likely to be compute" (2025-11-27). 100% reached May 2026 with Claude, GLM and Codex (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48284494).
- **Chromatron (2002).** Windows XP and PowerPC binaries; Ghidra (GhidrAssistMCP, then PyGhidra); Claude Code Opus 4.5→4.6; Cursor + GPT-5.2-Codex; pixelwise screenshot oracle; the Rust/Wasm build matches 307,199 of 307,200 pixels (https://quesma.com/blog/chromatron-recompiled/, 2026-03-04; https://p.migdal.pl/chromatron-oxide/).
- **Bumpy's Arcade Fantasy (1992 DOS).** "Claude-led" faithful decompilation grounded in Ghidra, built with Open Watcom, byte-compared to `BUMPY.EXE`, plus a playable `BUMPYP.EXE`; weekend → ~a month (update 2026-07-14); game files not distributed (https://github.com/GeReV/bumpy-reverse).
- **Klonoa: Empire of Dreams (GBA).** Claude Code wrote the Makefile, linker script and splitter and did most matching; 51% of code bytes; the agent tried to fork the compiler to force matches; it also found a real easter egg (https://gambiconf.substack.com/p/starting-a-decompilation-project, 2026-08-17).
- **Unnamed Windows FPS (private).** Claude Code + Codex CLIs with Hex-Rays ida-mcp; 99% of functions present, 83% byte-exact; "none of the code will be shared" (https://momo5502.com/posts/2026-10-09-game-decompilation/).
- **Melee** hit 100% ~2026-09-07 after six years, with LLMs credited for the final stretch (attribution inconsistent across outlets); devs call it "pretty unusable" as a PC port so far (https://www.eventhubs.com/news/2026/sep/19/super-smash-melee-decompiled-c/).
- **Binary-patch mods.** *InfinityUnlocked* (Disney Infinity 1.0) reportedly used Claude Code (Opus 4.6) + Ghidra for 17 exe patches and three data-file edits (secondary: https://www.popularai.org/p/ai-reverse-engineering-software-binaries, 2026-10-06).
- **Ports to new languages (secondary, self-reported).** reversedwithai.com lists 294 game projects (125 "Works"); Claude named in 249 of 273 model-attributed entries vs. GPT 37; examples: SSX 3 to Rust/wgpu, Micro Machines to TypeScript, GTA2 in-browser, Space Rangers to Delphi that "compiles to an identical 2.3 MB executable"; figures "are reported by project authors and not measured by this site" (https://reversedwithai.com/ai-game-decompilation, 2026-11-01). The awesome list adds Legends of Future Past rebuilt in Go/React "in one weekend," SkyRoads to Rust via Codex in ~6 hours, Tomba! (PS1) to native Windows in ~3 weeks — unverified timings (https://github.com/heaven-hm/awesome-reverse-engineered-games).
- **AI-written emulators.** A TypeScript Game Boy emulator "written solely by AI" (https://github.com/djkf/vibe-gameboy-emulator); a Claude-written NES emulator (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43535969, 2025-03).
- **Training on community data.** LLM4Decompile fine-tuned on ~23,400 "perfect" decomp.me N64 scratches (https://huggingface.co/blog/MatthewReingold/n64-decomp-dev-blog). Retro Reversing documents the standard stack (Ghidra, splat, m2c, asm-differ, decomp-permuter, decomp.me) and reports Opus 5.5 at 98.4% mean on five matching targets but only 37/200 on a behavioural "ProgramBench" (https://www.retroreversing.com/ai-assisted-decompilation, updated 2026-10-25).

### Community reaction (evidence)

decomp.dev closed its public index and leaderboard around 2026-10-15/16: "the community aspect of decompilation has been broken," leaderboards "warped decompilation into a hollow numbers game," and forks pursued "'port game to PC', all else be damned" — while declining to ban AI, which "can expedite the decompilation process in a significant way"; decomp.me stays up (https://www.timeextension.com/news/2026/10/the-community-aspect-of-decompilation-has-been-broken-decomp-dev-closes-in-the-wake-of-ai-based-bad-actors). N64Recomp's CONTRIBUTING.md: "AI must not be used to generate code for contributions to this project," citing provenance; Zelda64Recomp and BanjoRecomp blanket-ban GenAI (https://github.com/N64Recomp/N64Recomp/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md ; https://github.com/Zelda64Recomp/Zelda64Recomp). A browser GTA V port at playgta5.com vanished within hours of its 2026-10-06 launch, widely attributed to Take-Two though no notice was published (https://beebom.com/rockstar-shuts-down-free-gta-5-browser-port/).

### How the named idle games are built and how fans got the formulas (evidence)

- **Melvor Idle**: HTML5/TypeScript; Melvor Idle 2 is "written in C# utilizing the Godot game engine," published by Jagex as an official RuneScape game (https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/10/jagex-are-now-publishing-melvor-idle-2-as-an-official-runescape-game/, 2026-10). Official cross-platform mod support shipped in v1.1 (2022-10-18) via mod.io (https://testwiki.melvoridle.com/w/V1.1); mods are JavaScript (https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/Mod_Creation/Essentials); "over one million downloads on 140 mods" (https://www.moddb.com/games/melvor-idle/features/melvor-idle-surpasses-1-million-mod-downloads-powered-by-modio). Because the game object is live JS, fans built the **Melvor Idle Combat Simulator** (coolrox95 → Reloaded → [Myth]) that simulates monsters, dungeons and Slayer tasks and computes "Adjusted Clear Time" (https://github.com/coolrox95/Melvor-Idle-Combat-Simulator ; https://wiki.melvoridle.com/w/Combat_sim), plus the SEMI scripting engine (https://gitlab.com/sajadsjasdjsadj/SEMI).
- **Antimatter Dimensions**: fully open source, MIT, JavaScript + Vue, 12,166 commits (https://github.com/IvarK/AntimatterDimensionsSourceCode); every formula is in the code; a TypeScript save editor exists (https://github.com/Sato-Isolated/Antimatter-Dimensions-Save-Editor).
- **Cookie Clicker**: logic in one readable `main.js` ("© Orteil, 2013") served by the game page; the Cookie Monster addon overlays computed stats (https://github.com/CookieMonsterTeam/CookieMonster).
- **NGU Idle**: Unity/Mono on Steam; NGUInjector injects a DLL into the Mono process (original retired; forks e.g. https://github.com/rus9384/NGUInjector). Wiki editors verify formulas in the decompiled code — "Looked in the code: The wiki also mentions it" (https://reddit.birdcat.cafe/r/nguidle/comments/1jbgza2/trying_to_figure_out_the_math_of_basic_training, 2025-03-14).
- **Idle Slayer** (Pablo Leban, 2020): Unity IL2CPP; community wikis publish extracted curves such as "Total SP needed to raise n USP = 600000 × 1.23^(n−1)" (https://en.namu.wiki/w/Idle%20Slayer).
- **Magic Research** (Maticolotto): Electron, i.e., readable JavaScript (SteamDB above).

### Wiki datamining pipelines, and where LLMs fit

The mature pattern is scripted: parse game files → JSON → regenerate wiki Lua modules. Examples: Idle Champions tools "to update the ICotFR wiki using data parsed from game files" (https://github.com/txtsd/idlechampwiki); Warframe's "Automating Drop Tables" (https://warframe.fandom.com/wiki/WARFRAME_Wiki:Automating_Drop_Tables) and WFCD drop data (https://github.com/WFCD/warframe-drop-data); OSRS wiki dropsline data feeding RuneLite plugins (https://github.com/Frailrain/drop-rate-display). **I found no dated primary account of a wiki team using an LLM in its extraction pipeline** — an evidence gap, not proof of absence. Documented adjacent patterns: Karpathy's "LLM Wiki" (agent ingests sources, maintains interlinked markdown with index/log/lint; https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f, 2026-04-04); Universal Modder's `um scan` (2026-10-04); third-party Claude Code skills for BepInEx/Il2Cpp modding (https://mcpmarket.com/tools/skills/bepinex-game-modding, quality unverified). **Speculation:** the highest-value LLM uses for a datamining hobbyist are schema inference over piles of JSON/ScriptableObjects, plain-language explanation of decompiled C# formulas, and generating the parser plus wiki-table emitter — all checkable against in-game numbers.

## 4. Monetizable outputs that are legally defensible

**Companion apps and planners (evidence).** The big ones are open source or donation-funded: Path of Building Community is MIT (https://github.com/PathOfBuildingCommunity/PathOfBuilding); poe.ninja asks for support "via the Patreon button" (https://poe.ninja/support); Genshin Optimizer is MIT/TypeScript (https://github.com/frzyc/genshin-optimizer). Raidbots is the clearest paid model: Patreon → its own "Raidbots Premium" subscriptions, Patreon closed to new customers July 2023 (https://medium.com/raidbots/raidbots-premium-425e56dfcbdc ; https://support.raidbots.com/article/5-raidbots-premium-and-patreon). No public revenue figures exist for any of these.

**Wikis (evidence).** The Fandom exodus is driven by ads and content control: Wowpedia's 2023 vote cited Fandom becoming "more and more aggressive with ad insertion" (https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Forum:Vote_to_Leave_Fandom); the Minecraft Wiki moved to Weird Gloop (https://www.pcgamer.com/minecraft-wiki-completes-exit-from-fandom-gets-ready-to-fight-its-own-zombified-corpse); the GTA Wiki went independent in 2026 (https://kotaku.com/gta-wiki-leaving-fandom-rules-censorship-ads-videos-ditching-2000679115). Jagex gives the RuneScape wiki "some monetary and community support" plus a trademark license (Kotaku; https://meta.weirdgloop.org/). bg3.wiki is "non-commercial" with no ads (https://bg3.wiki/wiki/BG3Wiki:Copyright); the commercial counter-example, Fextralife, triggered Twitch's autoplay-embed policy change effective 2023-11-15 (https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/gothalion-says-twitch-ceo-plans-to-disable-autoplay-on-streamers-who-abuse-embeds-2319674/). **Speculation:** for a niche idle game a wiki is a traffic asset that feeds tools/Patreon, not a revenue line.

**Mod frameworks and paid mods (evidence, with revenue).**
- **Nexus Mods Donation Points:** 1 DP = $0.001, funded by Nexus itself, NET90 payout (https://help.nexusmods.com/article/112-donation-points-faq, 2024-11-07); the monthly pool was "most recently about $325,000" when the allocation algorithm changed in 2024 (https://www.nexusmods.com/news/14975). Nexus changed owners in June 2025 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nexus_Mods).
- **Overwolf/CurseForge:** "$240 million was paid out to in-game creators, including mod developers" in 2024 (https://blog.overwolf.com/in-game-creators-earn-big-overwolf-pays-out-240-million-in-2024/, 2025-01-26); ~$300M for 2025 per one outlet (provisional: https://games.gg/es/news/overwolf-creator-payouts-alcanzan-los-300-millones/).
- **Bethesda Creations** (2023-12-05; Verified Creators only): about **$10M** cumulative creator royalties, secondhand (https://www.tweaktown.com/news/112692/bethesdas-paid-mods-inspired-by-custom-d-d-campaigns-thats-the-spirit-of-creation/index.html ; rules at https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim_Mod:Verified_Creations).
- **Minecraft Marketplace:** >$350M gross by April 2021, Microsoft passing >50% to creators (https://www.pcgamer.com/au/minecraft-modders-have-sold-a-billion-mods-for-more-than-dollar350m-since-microsoft-bought-it); $500M+ cumulative claims lack a primary source.
- **mod.io Marketplace** (first UGC monetization service, 2024-08; https://docs.mod.io/monetization). Larian routed official BG3 mods through mod.io with Patch 7 (2024-09) and said it would never block modding outside the official pipeline (https://www.pcgamer.com/games/baldurs-gate/patch-7-of-baldur-s-gate-3-heralds-the-arrival-of-mod-support-in-september-with-a-closed-beta-starting-next-month).
- **Cautionary tale:** Valve's 2015 Skyrim paid mods (25% creator share) were pulled in four days — "it's clear we didn't understand exactly what we were doing" (https://www.pcgamer.com/valve-has-removed-paid-mods-functionality-from-steam-workshop).

**Save editors and translation patches.** Open-source single-player save editors are routine (Antimatter Dimensions editor above). Patch-only fan translations are widely believed lawful but "untested in court," and publishers have sent cease-and-desist letters (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fan_translation_of_video_games). **Speculation:** reputation builders, not revenue.

## 5. Legal landscape

| Case / law | Year | Holding | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Atari Games v. Nintendo, 975 F.2d 832 (Fed. **Policies and norms (evidence).** Take-Two/Rockstar (2017) "generally will not take legal action against third-party projects... that are single-player, non-commercial, and respect the intellectual property (IP) rights of third parties," excluding online modes (https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/rockstar-clarifies-i-grand-theft-auto-v-i-modding-policy). Capcom's R&D anti-cheat talk (2023-10): "All mods are defined as cheats, except when they are officially supported" (https://www.eventhubs.com/news/2023/nov/01/capcom-no-difference-mods-cheats/). Larian embraces mods via mod.io (2024); Nintendo pursues emulators and, indirectly, fan demakes (Portal 64). The "Stop Killing Games" ECI verified 1,294,188 signatures on 2026-01-24, forcing a Commission response (https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/final-count-for-stop-killing-games-petition-verifies-1-3-million-of-its-1-4-million-signatures-well-above-the-minimum-to-be-formally-considered-by-eu-politicians).

**Clean-room RE in practice.** Two separated teams: one studies the original and writes a code-free functional spec; the other, never exposed, implements from the spec — Phoenix's 1984 IBM-compatible BIOS method (https://historyofinformation.com/detail.php?id=3391 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean-room_design). It is best practice, not a legal requirement, and offers no patent defense. In 2026 the twist is that an LLM that has read the decompilation *is* the dirty team, so an AI reimplementation is clean-room only if the code-writing agent sees a spec, not the disassembly — the provenance objection N64Recomp raises.

**Where the lines are.** Defended: studying a game you own, extracting data for your own analysis, shipping your own tools or single-player mods containing none of the publisher's code or assets (Sega, Connectix, §1201(f), EU Art. 6, Rockstar's policy). Not defended: circumventing DRM or console encryption (Yuzu), reimplementing or running online services (BnetD, Nostalrius), selling tools for online games (Bossland), redistributing assets or decompiled code of a litigious publisher (re3). US EULAs can waive fair-use RE (Bowers, Davidson); EU/UK interoperability rights cannot be contracted away.

## 7. A lawful workflow for a solo idle-game developer

1. **Buy the game and read the EULA.** In the US a no-RE clause may bind (Bowers; Davidson); in the EU/UK, interoperability decompilation cannot be waived (Art. 8; s.296A). Skip games with kernel anti-cheat or always-online components; offline/single-player titles keep you on the Rockstar-policy side.
2. **Identify the engine** (SteamDB tech page; §3 signatures). Idle games are mostly HTML5/Electron (Melvor, Magic Research, Cookie Clicker, Antimatter Dimensions) or Unity (NGU Idle, Idle Slayer). Two of the most-studied incrementals are effectively open: Antimatter Dimensions is MIT; Cookie Clicker's `main.js` is served readable.
3. **Extract readable data, not assets.** C# via ILSpy/dnSpyEx, or Il2CppDumper + Ghidra/IDA with an MCP bridge; ScriptableObjects/JSON via AssetRipper/UABEA; Unreal DataTables via FModel; Godot via gdsdecomp; JS via source maps. Keep extracts local; never commit game data (Universal Modder's publish check and Retro Reversing's provenance advice exist for this).
4. **Use an LLM to document, then verify.** "Infer the schema of these JSON files"; "explain this decompiled method as a formula"; "write a parser that emits CSV." Treat outputs as hypotheses and check against in-game measurements (Chromatron) or a reference oracle (byte matching). Check provider data-handling terms before uploading proprietary binaries (Retro Reversing, 2026-10-25).
5. **Rebuild a simulator in your own code** — the Melvor Combat Simulator / Raidbots / Path of Building pattern: your expression of someone else's unprotectable rules. Use it to study XP curves, prestige multipliers, soft-caps and drop tables, and to calibrate your own pacing.
6. **Decide what to publish.** Yes: your simulator or planner, wiki text you wrote, derived formulas and tables, a mod shipping only your code for a publisher that permits single-player mods. No: extracted art/audio/text, decompiled source of a litigious publisher, anything touching DRM, keys or online play. Use the game's name descriptively with an "unofficial, not affiliated" notice (as bg3.wiki does). Disclose AI use — some communities ban it (N64Recomp), and decomp.dev's shutdown shows the social cost of ignoring norms.
7. **Record the reuse rules in the README.** Ideas, rules and formulas are not copyrightable (Copyright Office); specific expression — code, art, text, UI look-and-feel (Tetris, Spry Fox) and trademarks — is; mechanics may be patented (Pocketpair), so a direct clone of a patented interaction is a separate risk even if clean-room.

**Best monetizable angle this evidence supports (speculation, flagged):** a companion simulator/planner for an offline or mod-friendly idle game, free tier plus a Raidbots-style paid compute/sync tier, fed by formulas you derived and verified, with an independent wiki and an official-mod-platform presence (mod.io for Melvor-like games) as the traffic engine. Paid-mod marketplaces (Creations, CurseForge, Minecraft) pay real money but require platform admission and a willing publisher; the planner route depends only on shipping your own code and keeping the publisher's assets out of it.

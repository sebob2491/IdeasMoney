# Browser Recreations, Mashups and AI Coding Agents (2025–2026): Methods, Money and Legal Lines

Research date: 2026-11-01. Scope: how people used Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, Gemini and Grok in 2025–2026 to build browser recreations of well-known games and mashups; the platforms (UEFN, Roblox, s&box) where recreations get built and paid; monetization; legal exposure; and whether one person plus an agent can make money this way. Every figure is tagged **self-reported** (creator's own post), **platform-reported** (company press/docs, unaudited) or **third-party estimate**. No audited (SEC-level) per-game revenue exists for any browser game cited here; Roblox and Epic totals are company statements.

---

## 1. What people actually built (2025–2026)

**The template case: fly.pieter.com (Feb–Mar 2025).** Pieter Levels prompted Cursor ("Make a 3d flying game in browser with skyscrapers"), used Grok 3 for the WebSocket server, and shipped a vanilla Three.js multiplayer flight sim in roughly three hours ([Promptway, June 24, 2026](https://promptway.com/blog/pieter-levels-flight-sim-to-zero)). Monetization was in-game advertising, not players paying: on March 3, 2025 he posted "19x Blimps sold at various prices = $38,000/mo + 12x F16's sold @ $29.99 = ~$360" and "In-game ads are now $5,000/mo" ([X, Mar 3, 2025](https://x.com/levelsio/status/1896690611257844116), self-reported); on March 6 "$67,000 MRR" ([X, Mar 6, 2025](https://x.com/levelsio/status/1897784027186446820), self-reported); then a record 22,000 players in a day with "6 slots left and then it's full" ([X, Mar 2025](https://x.com/levelsio/status/1898894090466427386)). The widely repeated "$1M ARR in 17 days" is $87K MRR extrapolated. By mid-2026 his public bio listed the game at $0/month; his own project list files it under "Okay: Took off, Made money but not sustainable" ([levels.io/projects](https://levels.io/projects), accessed Nov 1, 2026). The revenue was one-off sponsor slots sold into a viral attention spike, not recurring player spend. The durable business that came out of it is the jam: Levels' bio in mid-2026 listed Vibej.am at $39K/month (self-reported via [Promptway](https://promptway.com/blog/pieter-levels-flight-sim-to-zero)).

**Vibe Jam 2025 (March–April 2025).** Over 1,000 entries, judged by Karpathy, Tim Soret, mrdoob, s13k_ and Levels; prizes $10,000/$5,000/$2,500. Winners: *The Great Taxi Assignment* (Tomas Bencko, a GTA-style taxi sim built with Cursor + Claude 3.7 Sonnet), *Vibeware*, *Vector Tango* ([levels.io, Apr 23, 2025](https://levels.io/winners-of-the-2025-vibe-code-game-jam)). Levels said he and s13k_ cut 1,000+ entries to ~500 "based on rules (many games didn't even load)" ([X, Apr 2025](https://x.com/levelsio/status/1907903828437144026)). The organizer noted most participants had never made a game before.

**Vibe Jam 2026 (Apr 1–May 1, 2026).** Rules: at least 90% AI-written code, web-playable, free, no login, no loading screens, Three.js recommended, multiplayer preferred; $40,000 in main prizes; sponsors Cursor, Bolt.new, Glif, Tripo ([vibejam.com/2026/press](https://vibejam.com/2026/press)). Winners announced June 17, 2026: *A Game About Capybaras Delivering Food* ($25,000; the author's Medium post title says it was built entirely with Claude Code in 15 days), *Fanto's Mega-Mart* ($10,000; Codex) and *WenWare* ($5,000; Codex, also "Most Played") ([levels.io, June 17, 2026](https://levels.io/vibe-jam-2026-winners-quality); tool attributions from [awesome-ai-built-games](https://github.com/lappemic/awesome-ai-built-games)). Judge Tim Soret: "The quality is much higher level than last year," predicting entries would match "a third-year student" within six to twelve months but not the top 20% of studios. Note the money flow: the only documented cash from these games is prize money, i.e., sponsor marketing budgets.

**"Claude of Duty" (Matt Shumer, late July 2026).** A Three.js r180 FPS with ~55k lines across 11 subsystems, "written by a fleet of AI agents under orchestration," zero external assets (procedural textures, meshes, synthesized Web Audio), MIT-licensed; 3.5k GitHub stars by Nov 1, 2026 ([github.com/mshumer/Claude-of-Duty](https://github.com/mshumer/Claude-of-Duty)). The README is candid: adversarial critic agents scored it 3.59→5.05/10 and every critic picked the real Call of Duty frame in a blind A/B; real gameplay ran 12–17 fps before optimization (28–30 after). Shumer corrected "one-shot" to "zero-shot"; a commenter claimed it was iterated over several days ([Enterprise DNA, July 27, 2026](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/ai-pulse/ai-pulse-2026-07-27-claude-of-duty-one-shot-fps-demo-gets-a-skeptical-reality-ch/)). It spawned a copycat wave built around his "Gauntlet Loop" builder/critic pattern ([Enterprise DNA, July 28, 2026](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/ai-pulse/ai-pulse-2026-07-28-matt-shumer-s-single-prompt-three-js-fps-claude-of-duty-trig/)). No monetization; a trademark-punning title; not taken down as of Nov 1, 2026.

**The CoD Zombies browser wave and the DMCA (Sept–Oct 2026).** Developer Luckey Faraday, who builds with AI coding agents, announced a Three.js remake of the Black Ops map *Moon* on Sept 14, 2026, bundled it on Oct 9 with *Kino der Toten* (rounds, barricades, perks, Mystery Box, Pack-a-Punch) and *Claude of Duty: Vibe Slops II* (Black Ops 2 free-for-all vs six bots on Hijacked and Nuketown 2025). Activision's DMCA notices arrived Oct 10, 2026: games offline, posts removed, X account locked; the GitHub repo is gone ([PortedGames, checked Oct 17, 2026](https://portedgames.com/moon-zombies/); [GameRiv, Oct 11, 2026](https://gameriv.com/activision-reportedly-takes-down-call-of-duty-browser-projects-with-dmca-notice/); [TweakTown, Oct 2026](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/114029/activision-takes-down-ai-made-black-ops-2-browser-ports/index.html)). The decisive fact: the project pages stated that maps, models and animations were exported from the original PC games ([browsergame.si, Oct 19, 2026](https://browsergame.si/articles/call-of-duty-zombies-in-the-browser)). Faraday's own framing: "Activision just DMCA'd me for porting CoD games to the browser… It was fun while it lasted" ([X](https://x.com/luckeyfaraday/status/2108722723849379986)). A separate *Black Ops 1 Zombies* port at vel.gg, built on a "decompiled engine code" repo created Oct 4, 2026 (315 stars) with all ten maps and a community co-op Chrome extension, was still reachable on Nov 1, 2026, though one tracker reports its original path began returning HTTP 451 on Oct 19 and moved ([github.com/MisaDev4/bo1-zombies-decompiled](https://github.com/MisaDev4/bo1-zombies-decompiled); [browsergame.si](https://browsergame.si/articles/call-of-duty-zombies-in-the-browser); [PortedGames index, Nov 1, 2026](https://portedgames.com/)). PortedGames tracks 66 browser ports, of which 15 are offline or taken down.

**Mechanics-only demakes exist but have no audience.** *call-of-the-undead* (vanilla Three.js, no build step, runtime-generated textures, synthesized SFX, Nacht-inspired map, perks/PaP/Mystery Box, MIT, "Fan project, not affiliated with Activision") and *Jayhub27/cod-zombies* (2D canvas + Three.js, "zero external assets") each sit at 0 stars ([call-of-the-undead](https://github.com/malekthegamer/call-of-the-undead); [cod-zombies](https://github.com/Jayhub27/cod-zombies)). They still use Activision's perk names and map names, which is the remaining exposure.

**The legal template: mrdoob's three-quake.** The Three.js creator ported Quake to Three.js "with @claude," under GPL v2, shipping only the shareware episode and telling users to supply their own pak0 for the full game; 340 stars, live demo ([github.com/mrdoob/three-quake](https://github.com/mrdoob/three-quake)). GPL engine + shareware data + user-supplied retail data is the one proven safe pattern for ports of commercial games.

**Minecraft clones.** A 19-year-old built a Three.js voxel clone with Claude Code in 92 prompts over ~11 hours, including an InstancedMesh fix from 3 fps to 60 fps ([note.com, Mar 25, 2026](https://note.com/daijyu_dev/n/ncd2ec59c7c1c)). Dozens of Three.js Minecraft clones are public ([dgreenheck/minecraft-threejs-clone](https://github.com/dgreenheck/minecraft-threejs-clone)); no Microsoft takedown of one surfaced in this research.

## 2. Stacks and their maturity for agent-written web games

- **Three.js (+ Rapier physics).** r180 shipped early Sept 2025 and r185 by July 2026 ([mirror](https://sourceforge.net/projects/three-js.mirror/files/r180); [Sensei Notes, Jul 2026](https://buttondown.com/wawasensei/archive/sensei-notes-11-threejs-r185-game-ready-ai-models/)). It dominates the agent-built catalog: of 95 games in the awesome-ai-built-games list, roughly half are Three.js, with Rapier (Rust→WASM physics) in the multiplayer ones such as *Rose & Blade* (16 players) and *Turbo Rumble* ([list](https://github.com/lappemic/awesome-ai-built-games)). Hugging Face's Dylan Ebert tried the same game on Roblox (MCP), Unity (unofficial MCP) and three.js/rapier/bitecs with Claude Code: "the web stack gave the best AI performance, likely due to abundant web development training data," but "one-shot 'make me a game' prompting doesn't work," so he shipped a declarative ECS engine with an `llms.txt` for agents ([HF blog, Sept 29, 2025](https://huggingface.co/blog/vibegame)). An April 2025 postmortem found Claude 3.5 best in Cursor because other models "get stuck in fix loops" ([X, Apr 2025](https://x.com/0x_ultra/status/1910214561845932213)). No formal Three.js agent benchmark exists; jam results and Shumer's self-measured 12–17 fps are the best quantitative data.
- **Babylon.js 8.0** (Mar 27, 2025) moved all core shaders to WGSL, added IBL shadows and area lights ([forum announcement](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/babylon-js-8-0-is-officially-here/57452)); Shell Shockers is Babylon.js ([GameDiscoverCo, Jan 30, 2023](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/deep-dive-shell-shockers-multi-million)).
- **PlayCanvas** renders WebGL 2 by default with opt-in WebGPU and automatic fallback ([engine docs](https://developer.playcanvas.com/user-manual/engine/supported-browsers.md)); Venge.io was built in it.
- **Phaser 4** went stable in April 2026 (v4.1.0; 4.2.1 current) as a WebGL rewrite, not WebGPU; Phaser's WebGPU engine "Phaser AE" is separate, closed-source and MCP-connectable ([releases](https://github.com/phaserjs/phaser/releases); [phaser.io, Jul 2026](https://phaser.io/news/2026/07/no-you-didn-t-miss-a-3d-update)).
- **Godot 4.** Since 4.3 single-threaded export is "the preferred and now default way" to ship on the web; threaded builds need `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin` and `Cross-Origin-Embedder-Policy: require-corp` (i.e., no third-party ads or iframes on the host page) or a PWA service-worker workaround; C# "currently cannot be exported to the web"; WebGL 2 only ([docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html); [4.3 progress report](https://godotengine.org/article/progress-report-web-export-in-4-3/)). That header requirement is exactly why single-thread became default: portals like Poki and CrazyGames run ads.
- **Unity 6.1** (Apr 2025) added WebGPU as an experimental backend; WebGL 2 remains default ([Unity manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.1/Documentation/Manual/WebGPU.html)); third-party reports say 6.6 (Sept 2026) dropped the experimental label (unverified against Unity).
- **Unreal** has no official HTML5 target in UE5; Wonder Interactive's SimplyStream publishes UE 5.6/5.8 to WebGPU as a third-party service ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/?p=320048); [forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/webgpu-for-unreal-engine-5-5-6-and-5-7-support/2693702)).
- **Bevy** 0.17 shipped Sept 30, 2025 ([release notes](https://bevy.org/news/bevy-0-17/)); web builds remain single-threaded because wgpu types are not Send/Sync ([PR #12205](https://www.github.com/bevyengine/bevy/pull/12205/files)).

Practical reading: agents write Three.js/vanilla JS best; Godot single-thread is the strongest "engine with editor" option for portals; Unity/Unreal web paths carry large bundles and header/plugin friction that jam rules ("no loading screens") penalize.

## 3. Decompilation-to-browser ports and emulation

- **Doom/Quake (safe).** id re-licensed Doom's engine under GPL v2 on Oct 3, 1999 ([DoomWiki](https://www.doomwiki.org/wiki/Doom_source_code)); Quake's engine is GPL and "the Quake data files remain copyrighted and can't be redistributed," with the shareware episode downloadable but restricted ([id-Software/Quake](https://github.com/id-Software/Quake)). Freedoom provides a free replacement IWAD ([DoomWiki](https://doomwiki.org/wiki/Freedoom)). Browser ports (Wasm Doom, three-quake, Levels' q1/q2/q3.pieter.com source ports) are therefore engine-legal; data legality depends on using shareware/free data or user-supplied files.
- **Super Mario 64 (unsafe).** Nintendo had the 2015 Unity browser level removed ([GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/articles/nintendo-forces-takedown-of-super-mario-64-hd-brow/1100-6426295/)) and in 2020 used Wildwood Law Group to DMCA the decomp-based PC port and videos ([Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/117984/working-super-mario-64-pc-port-hit-by-nintendo-copyright-takedowns-of-course)). A JS/WASM port repo exists with Apache-2.0 code and 4 stars ([lwllac/sm64](https://www.github.com/lwllac/sm64)); the decompiled code is "clean" but any shipped build contains Nintendo assets.
- **Zelda OoT/MM (gray, untested).** Ship of Harkinian and Zelda 64: Recompiled (N64Recomp, May 2024) ship no assets and require the user's own ROM; Nintendo has not acted against them as of the coverage found, but the legality of extracting a ROM "varies by country and has never been comprehensively settled" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_of_Harkinian); [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/adventure/a-new-native-port-of-majoras-mask-might-change-how-we-play-n64-games-on-our-pcs)).
- **AI in decompilation.** Community agents now combine Claude with splat, m2c, asm-differ and the IDO compiler, and drive Ghidra via MCP ([Retro Reversing](https://www.retroreversing.com/ai-assisted-decompilation)); a Hugging Face write-up fine-tuning LLM4Decompile on N64 reported it "did not work well" ([HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/MatthewReingold/n64-decomp-dev-blog)); the LLM4Decompile paper's gains are on x86 Linux, not consoles ([EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.203.pdf)). The Oct 2026 Black Ops "decompiled engine" repo does not state whether AI was used.
- **Emulation.** EmulatorJS is RetroArch compiled with Emscripten ([RomM docs](https://docs.romm.app/4.4.0/Platforms-and-Players/EmulatorJS-Player/)); emulators are lawful under Sony v. Connectix (9th Cir. 2000), ROM distribution is not ([Fordham IPLJ](https://fordhamiplj.org/2016/11/21/legality-emulation/)). Nintendo now targets people: Yuzu settled for $2.4M with a permanent injunction in March 2024 ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/switch-emulator-yuzu-reaches-2-4-million-settlement-with-nintendo)); Ryujinx shut down in Oct 2024 after Nintendo "contacted its lead developer" ([VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/switch-emulator-ryujinx-has-been-taken-down-after-nintendo-contacted-its-lead-developer)).

## 4. Platform recreations: UEFN, Roblox, Garry's Mod/s&box

**UEFN economics.** Epic pays 40% of Fortnite's eligible net revenue into an engagement pool ([Tubefilter, June 17, 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/06/17/epic-games-unreal-editor-for-fortnite-creator-payouts/)). Platform-reported totals: $320M in the first year (Konvoy, [June 28, 2024](https://www.konvoy.vc/newsletters/uefn-a-sleeping-giant)); $352M in 2024 ([esports.gg](https://esports.gg/news/fortnite/fortnite-paid-352-million-out-to-map-creators-in-2024-the-companys-highest-creator-payout-yet/)); $722M cumulative across 11.2B hours and 260K islands (Epic, [Sept 2025](https://www.fortnite.com/news/fortnite-developers-will-soon-be-able-to-sell-in-game-items)); over $1B cumulative by June 2026, with ~3,000 islands now earning more from in-island item sales than from engagement ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/06/17/epic-games-unreal-editor-for-fortnite-creator-payouts/)). Distribution is the key number, from Epic's own GDC 2024 disclosure: 23.5k creators; 4% earned $20k+/year (vs 0.1% on Roblox, ~15% on Steam); the top 100 took 71%; nine developers took 40%; 40 earned over $1M; the top earner (pandvil) made ~$20.4M ([Game World Observer, Apr 1, 2024](https://gameworldobserver.com/?p=23839)). On Nov 1, 2025 the formula changed: only engagement from players who have made purchases counts, retention is island-specific, and creators who bring new or returning players get 75% of that player's pool contribution for six months ([Epic forum, Nov 1, 2025](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/updated-engagement-payout-formula-with-user-acquisition-rewards/2670742)). Community calculators put payouts at roughly $0.02–$0.05 per engaged hour (third-party estimates, [creatorxp.gg](https://creatorxp.gg/guides/uefn-engagement-payouts-explained)); the payout threshold is $100.

**UEFN IP policy and the CoD precedent.** Epic's 2023 guidance: "Even if third party intellectual property otherwise exists in Fortnite… you do not have the right to use that content to create your island without a separate license from that third party," and Epic may share your name and address with a claimant ([Epic, Mar 22, 2023](https://www.fortnite.com/news/intellectual-property-ip-and-dmca-guidelines-for-fortnite-island-creators); Island Creator Rules updated [Aug 5, 2025](https://www.fortnite.com/news/fortnite-island-creator-rules)). In April 2023 Activision DMCA'd Creative 2.0 recreations of Rust and Shipment after a clip went viral ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/fortnite-creative-call-of-duty-maps-dmca-takedown/)). Round-based zombies islands are small: one shows 27.1K plays and an all-time peak of 216 players ([Fortnite.GG](https://fortnite.gg/island/7227-0095-1933)); *Zombie Farm* peaked above 3,600 concurrent when featured in Discovery ([Sportskeeda, ~2024](https://www.sportskeeda.com/fortnite/fortnite-zombie-farm-uefn-map-code-play)). Nothing CoD-branded survives discovery.

**Roblox economics.** DevEx standard rate rose to $0.0038 per earned Robux on Sept 5, 2025 ([rate history](https://github.com/supertj/devex-rates)); a US 18+ rate of $0.0054 applies from June 8, 2026 to spend by age-checked US adults in R15-only games ([Roblox docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/monetization/18-plus-devex-rate); [newsroom, Apr 30, 2026](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/04/roblox-fuels-high-fidelity-games-over-18-players-increases-qualifying-devex-rate-42)). Creators earned over $1.5B via DevEx in 2025 (platform-reported, same release). Concentration (third-party estimate, ROLearn, Feb 6, 2026): top 1,000 developers ~73% of payouts, averaging ~$1.1M; ranks 1,001–10,000 average ~$28k ([ROLearn](https://rolearn.dev/trend-reports/creator-economy-2025-report)). Epic's 0.1%-earn-$20k+ comparison above is the only platform-attributed distribution figure.

**Roblox IP.** Rights Manager lets any verified rights holder, Roblox presence or not, file removal requests against experiences, avatar items and assets ([docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/rights-manager)); Pokémon Brick Bronze was deleted on The Pokémon Company's DMCA on Apr 18, 2018 ([fan wiki](https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/Roblox_Copyright_and_trademark_infringement)). Roblox's July 15, 2025 License Manager (Netflix, Sega, Lionsgate, Kodansha) and Nov 2025 self-serve expansion (1,500+ license requests) create a legal path for branded recreations, but only as full-experience adaptations approved by the rights holder ([Roblox newsroom, Jul 2025](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/07/roblox-launches-new-licensing-platform-for-experiences); [Nov 2025](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/11/roblox-self-serve-ip-licensing)). Meanwhile the CoD-Zombies-mechanics genre thrives unlicensed with original art: *Project Lazarus: Zombies* (created June 28, 2016) shows 219.8M visits, ~1K concurrent and 1.9M favorites on Nov 1, 2026 ([Bloxodes](https://bloxodes.com/stats/games/project-lazarus-zombies-169302362)); *Michael's Zombies* is in the same range (secondary). Neither has been taken down in a decade: rounds, points, perks, wall-buys and a mystery box are mechanics.

**Garry's Mod and s&box.** Nintendo forced Facepunch to purge 20 years of Nintendo Workshop items in April 2024 ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/sim/garrys-mod-is-removing-20-years-worth-of-nintendo-related-items-from-its-steam-workshop-following-takedown-request-its-nintendo-need-more-be-said)). s&box (Source 2, C#) released on Steam Apr 28, 2026 after MIT-licensing its code in Nov 2025; a March 2026 Valve license lets creators export and sell standalone Steam games royalty-free; an in-platform "Play Fund" pays creators (amounts unpublished); price was cut from $20 to $10, made free Oct 3–5, 2026, and Facepunch says it will go free-to-play; a May 2026 update down-ranks games with generative-AI thumbnails ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%26box); [iXBT, Oct 3, 2026](https://ixbt.games/en/news/2026/10/03/naslednicu-garrys-mod-sdelali-vremenno-besplatnoi-i-snizili-bazovuiu-cenu-v-steam.html)). It is the most permissive mashup platform on paper, with the smallest audience.

## 5. World-model "generated games": research, demo or product?

- **Oasis (Decart/Etched, Oct 31, 2024):** Minecraft-like diffusion world model; 500M weights and inference code released, larger model demo-only, "no monetization" ([oasis-model.github.io](https://oasis-model.github.io); [open-oasis](https://github.com/etched-ai/open-oasis)). **Oasis 2.0 (Sept 5, 2025)** is real-time style transfer over a running Minecraft via a server-dependent Fabric mod, not a game generator ([Decart on X](https://x.com/DecartAI/status/1963758685995368884); [Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/oasis2)). **Mirage (July 17, 2025)** is live video-to-video at <40 ms ([Kfir Aberman on X](https://x.com/AbermanKfir/status/1945950087810888029)); Decart raised $100M at $3.1B in Aug 2025 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/07/exclusive-decart-raises-100-million-at-a-3-1-billion-valuation-chasing-the-future-of-real-time-creative-ai/)). **Oasis 3 (June 2026)** is driving simulation sold by API at $0.02/second ([AI Directory](https://aidirectory.com/news/decart-launches-oasis-3-driving-world-model-api)). Decart is the only vendor with developer-facing APIs, and they do restyling and simulation, not playable game generation.
- **GameNGen (Google, ICLR 2025):** Doom at 20+ fps on one TPU via Stable Diffusion 1.4 conditioned on frames and actions; research only ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.14837v2)); a March 2026 follow-up adds multiplayer Doom rollouts ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.06679)).
- **Microsoft Muse/WHAM (Feb 2025, Nature):** 200M and 1.6B checkpoints released under the Microsoft Research License, "intended for academic research purposes only," 300×180 px, ~10 s context, "too slow for real-time use" ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/wham)). The Copilot Labs Quake II demo (Apr 2025) was a minutes-long, widely panned feasibility study ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/06/microsoft-releases-ai-generated-quake-ii-demo-but-admits-limitations/)).
- **Google Genie 3 (Aug 5, 2025):** 720p/24 fps, a few minutes of consistency, one-minute visual memory, limited research preview ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-3-a-new-frontier-for-world-models/)); Project Genie opened to AI Ultra subscribers on Jan 29, 2026 with 60-second sessions ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genie_(world_model))). **Genie 4 (Oct 27, 2026):** native audio, real-time language steering, physics; research preview for trusted testers with Gemini API early access "in the coming weeks"; objects still drift over long runs ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-4-a-new-generation-of-world-simulation/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/27/googles-genie-4-world-model-adds-audio-more-realistic-worlds-and-real-time-steering/)).

Verdict: nothing here is a platform a solo developer can ship a game on in 2026. Hosted per-second inference economics also make ad-supported browser play impossible at $0.02/s ($72/player-hour).

## 6. Asset pipeline for a solo developer

- **CC0 libraries:** Poly Haven states all HDRIs, textures and models are CC0 and may be bundled in products you sell ([license](https://polyhaven.com/license)); Kenney ([kenney.nl/assets](https://www.kenney.nl/assets)) and Quaternius ([GameFromScratch](https://gamefromscratch.com/quaternius-free-3d-assets/)) publish CC0 packs (verify the license file in each pack). Sketchfab's store closed into Fab in Oct 2024 and free licensing was slated to end on Sketchfab during 2025, leaving models view-only ([Sketchfab blog](https://sketchfab.com/blogs/community/sketchfab-update-what-you-need-to-know-now-that-fabs-live/)); current status is murky and a single source reports a KitBash acquisition in Aug 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sketchfab)). Treat Sketchfab as unreliable going forward.
- **Mixamo** is still free with an Adobe ID but unmaintained (FAQ last updated 2021; Blender add-on outdated) ([guide, 2026](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/free-character-animations-rigging)).
- **AI 3D:** Meshy (free outputs CC BY; paid ~$14.50–$30/mo), Tripo (~$19.90/mo Pro; Mixamo-compatible rigs), Hyper3D Rodin (hosted only), Hunyuan3D 2.1 (open weights June 13, 2025; license excludes EU/UK/KR), Microsoft TRELLIS.2 (MIT, Dec 16, 2025); Luma Genie appears withdrawn ([Sloyd comparison](https://sloyd.ai/blog/3d-ai-price-comparison); [timeline](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/ai-3d-model-generation-timeline-2026)). Props come out well; rigged, animated enemies remain the bottleneck.
- **Audio:** ElevenLabs Sound Effects (June 2024, trained with Shutterstock; commercial use on paid tiers) ([Voicebot](https://voicebot.ai/2024/06/03/elevenlabs-launches-generative-ai-text-to-sound-effects-tool/)); Suno Pro $10 / Premier $30 grant commercial rights to tracks made while subscribed, with ownership language changed in Dec 2025 ([Dynamoi](https://dynamoi.com/learn/ai-music-distribution/suno-commercial-rights-explained)).
- **Steam disclosure:** Valve's Jan 2026 rewrite exempts AI coding tools but still requires disclosure of any generated content players see or hear, pre-generated or live ([VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/valve-has-significantly-rewritten-steams-rules-for-how-developers-much-disclose-ai-use)); ~8,000 Steam titles disclosed generative AI in H1 2025 ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/valve-tweaks-and-clarifies-ai-disclosure-rules-for-steam)).
- **Ripped assets:** tools to extract models from commercial games exist; this report does not describe them. The consequence is documented above: Faraday's projects, which disclosed that assets came from the retail games, were offline within 26 days of the bundled launch, with his social account locked ([PortedGames](https://portedgames.com/moon-zombies/)).

## 7. Multiplayer for browser games and cost at scale

- **Colyseus** (Node.js rooms, used by several CoD-style browser prototypes): open source; Colyseus Cloud from $15/month with no CCU/DAU caps ([pricing](https://www.colyseus.io/pricing)).
- **Nakama** is open source to self-host; Heroic Cloud is sized by CPU with "No DAU/MAU/CCU limits" and no public price (third parties cite ~$600/month floors) ([Heroic Labs](https://heroiclabs.com/pricing)).
- **Photon** (Fusion/PUN; WebGL supported via WebSockets): 20 CCU free; 100 CCU $95 one-time for 12 months; 500 CCU $95/mo; 1,000 CCU $185/mo; 2,000 CCU $370/mo; Premium $0.29/CCU with a $580/mo minimum ([pricing](https://www.photonengine.com/pun/pricing)).
- **PlayroomKit:** hosted state sync for Three.js, Phaser, PixiJS, PlayCanvas, Godot and Unity with "a generous free tier"; limits and prices are on a billing page not readable in this research ([docs](https://docs.joinplayroom.com/)).
- **Cloudflare Durable Objects** (one object per room, WebSocket hibernation): Workers Paid $5/mo minimum; $0.15 per million requests after 1M; $12.50 per million GB-s after 400k; incoming WebSocket messages billed at 20:1; hibernating sockets incur no duration; a room handling 1.5M requests and 1M active seconds costs about $5.15/month ([Cloudflare docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/)). For a 4-player co-op wave shooter this is the cheapest authoritative-server path.
- Scale reference: Shell Shockers' backend "must handle 10,000+ simultaneous players" at 300–350k DAU ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/deep-dive-shell-shockers-multi-million)); Levels' flight sim ran a single Grok-written WebSocket server for 16–22k daily players (self-reported).

## 8. Monetization and distribution evidence

Two models have produced seven-figure annual revenue for browser games, and neither is "sell the game": (1) **ad-supported, kid-heavy portal and school traffic** (Shell Shockers: 80–90% ads, 39% of players on Chromebooks, mostly ages 10–15, "low seven figures annually," founder-reported, Jan 2023 ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/deep-dive-shell-shockers-multi-million))); (2) **cosmetic economies at very large scale** (Krunker: 200M+ unique players before FRVR acquired it, terms undisclosed, May 12, 2022 ([press release](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/frvr-acquires-popular-free-to-play-shooter-krunkerio))). Venge.io's three founders turned 50M+ Poki gameplays into a full-time studio without publishing figures ([Poki, Mar 3, 2022](https://poki.com/blog/meet-onrush-studio)). Levels' third model, **selling in-game ad slots directly to startups** at $5,000/month per slot (self-reported, Mar 2025), worked only during a viral window and decayed to zero.

Portal terms are opaque by design: Poki publishes deal types, not percentages, and defaults to web exclusivity ([Playgama, Sept 2026](https://playgama.com/blog/business-faqs/poki-vs-crazygames-vs-gamedistribution-revenue-share/)); CrazyGames requires its SDK, bans external ads, and pays monthly above €100 ([FAQ](https://docs.crazygames.com/faq/)); GameDistribution's 33% is the only contractually published share; itch.io defaults to 10% ([itch.io](https://itch.io/docs/creators/payments)); Kongregate closed to new games on July 1, 2020 ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/browser-game-portal-kongregate-is-no-longer-accepting-new-games)); Newgrounds' current rev-share terms could not be verified, and its wiki describes rev-share in the past tense ([Newgrounds wiki](https://www.newgrounds.com/wiki/help-information/content-submission/project-system)). The web-to-Steam path is proven: Vampire Survivors was a Phaser game posted on itch.io "with little engagement," entered Steam Early Access in Dec 2021 and hit 1.0 on Oct 20, 2022 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vampire_Survivors)); Phaser documents the Electron pipeline ([Phaser, Mar 26, 2025](https://phaser.io/news/2025/03/publishing-web-games-on-steam-with-electron)); Steam Direct costs $100 per app, recoupable at $1,000 gross ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/appfee)).

## 9. Legal landscape: what is protected and who enforces

**The line.** The US Copyright Office: "Copyright does not protect the idea for a game, its name or title, or the method or methods for playing it," while artwork, text and audio in a game are protectable ([FL-108](https://www.copyright.gov/fls/fl108.pdf); [ABA Landslide](https://americanbar.org/groups/intellectual_property_law/publications/landslide/2014-15/march-april/its_how_you_play_game_why_videogame_rules_are_not_expression_protected_copyright_law)). Applied to CoD Zombies: round structure, points-for-kills, buying doors, wall weapons, perk effects, a randomized weapon box and a weapon-upgrade station are unprotected mechanics (which is why Project Lazarus has run since 2016). Map geometry and layout as rendered art, character and weapon models, textures, animations, voice lines, music, the names Kino der Toten/Juggernog/Pack-a-Punch/Mystery Box (copyright in text plus trademark/trade-dress exposure), and the marks Call of Duty and Black Ops are protected. Names and titles fall under trademark, not copyright, so "Claude of Duty" is a trademark question, not a copyright one.

**Enforcement patterns (dated):**
- Activision: Fortnite Creative 2.0 CoD map DMCAs (Apr 2023); cease-and-desist killing the H2M Modern Warfare 2 mod one day before its Aug 16, 2024 launch ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/fps/activision-kills-ambitious-h2m-modern-warfare-2-mod-one-day-before-its-release)); DMCA of Faraday's AI-built browser ports (Oct 10, 2026). Pattern: fast, non-negotiable, triggered by virality, indifferent to non-commercial disclaimers.
- Nintendo: 562 GameJolt fan games and AM2R pulled (Aug–Sept 2016) ([BGR](https://www.bgr.com/entertainment/nintendo-dmca-fan-games-game-jolt/); [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2016/09/nintendo_of_america_issues_takedown_request_on_am2r_ending_the_project)); Pokémon Uranium after 1.5M downloads ([TheWrap](https://thewrap.com/pokemon-uranium-nintendo-go)); SM64 PC port (2020); Garry's Mod purge (Apr 2024); Yuzu (Mar 2024); Ryujinx (Oct 2024); Pokémon fan-shooter clips removed ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/nintendo-is-removing-videos-of-fan-game-that-had-players-shooting-pokemon)). Pattern: broadest and most aggressive, now reaching individuals.
- Take-Two: sued the re3/reVC GTA reverse-engineering authors in 2021, settled in principle and dismissed in 2023 under undisclosed terms ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/gta-owner-settles-lawsuit-against-creators-of-vice-city-reverse-engineering-project-before-case-reaches-court); [TorrentFreak](https://torrentfreak.com/take-two-dismisses-claims-against-lead-defendants-in-gta-mods-lawsuit-230405/)). Pattern: litigation even against clean-room code when the output competes with its own remasters.
- Valve: licensed Black Mesa commercially ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/q-a-re-imagining-1998-s-i-half-life-i-for-a-2020-audience-in-i-black-mesa-i-)) yet took down Team Fortress: Source 2 and the Portal 64 demake ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/after-years-of-supporting-mods-and-fan-games-valve-takes-action-against-team-fortress-and-portal-fan-projects)). Pattern: tolerant, with exceptions tied to Nintendo-adjacent risk and Source 2 licensing.
- Sega: "no issue with Sonic fan games as long as they are not monetized" (May 2021) and hired fan-game developer Christian Whitehead for Sonic Mania ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2021/05/sega_is_still_cool_with_sonic_fan_games_provided_no_profit_is_involved); [Bloomberg, 2017](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2017-08-09/sega-embraces-rogue-fan-programmers-with-retro-sonic-game-sequel)). Pattern: tolerance conditioned on no monetization, which is the opposite of this report's goal.

**Platform terms** add contractual exposure on top of copyright: Epic requires a separate license for any third-party IP and may hand your identity to the claimant; Roblox's Rights Manager lets any rights holder remove experiences; both can withhold payouts.

## 11. Verdict for one person plus an agent

**(a) Original-IP browser wave-survival co-op shooter — viable as a portfolio and ad-revenue play, not a quick win.** Build difficulty is low-to-moderate: the CoD Zombies loop (rounds, points, doors, wall-buys, perks, random box, upgrade station) is legally free to copy and two zero-asset Three.js demakes already implement it; four-player multiplayer costs ~$5–15/month on Durable Objects or Colyseus Cloud; CC0 and generated props cover environments, with rigged enemies the real cost. Discoverability is the problem: those demakes have zero stars, Vibe Jam fields 1,000 games a month, and even winners earn only prize money. The comparables that make money (Shell Shockers, Krunker, Venge) are original-IP shooters that reached hundreds of thousands of daily players through portals and schools over years, 80–90% of it ads on a 10–15-year-old audience. Realistic path: ship single-threaded on CrazyGames/Poki with their SDK, accept a 33–50% share, and treat a Steam wrapper as upside. Expect low hundreds to low thousands per month unless it breaks out; fly.pieter.com's $0/month endpoint shows what attention-based ad sales become.

**(b) UEFN or Roblox experience — the only path with a working payout rail, with brutal concentration.** Epic's own 2024 data: 4% of 23.5k creators cleared $20k/year and 100 creators took 71% of $320M; since Nov 1, 2025 only paying players count, which community reports say cut payouts for creators with non-spending audiences. Roblox paid $1.5B in 2025, but third-party estimates give the top 1,000 about 73% and ranks 1,001–10,000 about $28k average, with Epic citing 0.1% of Roblox developers above $20k. A CoD-mechanics zombies game with original art is tolerated on both (Project Lazarus: 219.8M visits since 2016); anything CoD-branded is DMCA'd within weeks (Fortnite, Apr 2023). Agents help less here: Verse and Luau have thinner training data than JavaScript, and Ebert found Roblox the weakest agent target. Build cost is higher than Three.js; expected value is higher because distribution is built in.

**(c) Mashup genre game — the best risk-adjusted bet if the mashup is of mechanics, not brands.** Mechanics are uncopyrightable; two games' loops combined (e.g., round-based zombies plus roguelite upgrades, or Vampire Survivors-style auto-battler plus a flight sim) is how Vampire Survivors itself went from a Phaser browser game with "little engagement" to a Steam hit. The failure mode is importing either game's names, models, maps or audio, which converts a legal mashup into Faraday's outcome in under a month. s&box offers royalty-free Steam export for mashups built on Source 2 but has a small, newly free-to-play audience and no published creator payouts.

**Overall:** the methods are real and cheap (Three.js + Claude Code/Cursor/Codex, CC0 assets, Durable Objects or Colyseus, portal SDKs), the legal line is clear (mechanics yes, expression and marks no), and the money is thin and concentrated everywhere except at the top of UEFN and Roblox. The one documented six-figure monthly browser outcome in this period (Levels) was ad-slot sales into a founder's existing audience during a viral week, and it went to zero. A solo developer should plan on portfolio value plus low-four-figure monthly portal income from an original-IP mechanics clone, and treat UEFN/Roblox as the only route where a hit pays life-changing money.
